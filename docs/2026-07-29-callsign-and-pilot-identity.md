# Decision record: GHOST 11, and who is flying the Rapier

**Decision (2026-07-29):** the player's flight callsign is **GHOST 11**, spoken **"Ghost
Eleven"** (group form). Pattern traffic are the squadron's other slots: Ghost Twelve,
Thirteen, Fourteen. The *field* keeps the program name — Rapier Tower, Rapier Approach —
because station names come from the installation, not the aircraft. The callsign word
deliberately does **not** match the airframe: that mismatch is the realism (Death 41 flew an
F-16, Shooter 010 a T-6, KATT 11 a T-45; nobody flies "Falcon 11" in a Falcon).

## How callsigns are actually assigned (research base)

- **Format and allocation [OFFICIAL]:** USAF voice callsigns are spoken dictionary words of
  4–6 letters with a one- or two-digit suffix, allocated per unit with number series
  deconflicted across squadrons at a base (AFI 33-217 lineage; JO 7110.65 2-4-20 gives the
  ATC-facing format). GHOST: five letters, to-spec.
- **Two numbering regimes [OBSERVED]:** formation-positional numbers off the daily schedule
  (flight digit + position digit, spoken digit-by-digit — "Death four one") versus static
  squadron slots (the number as standing identity, spoken group form — "Viper eleven", owner
  testimony). No retrievable pub writes the pronunciation rule; recorded as observed practice.
  The Rapier force flies single-ship from dispersed tubes: static slots, group form.
- **Ukrainian practice [OBSERVED]:** UkrAF callsign culture is young and Western-adopted —
  Andrii "Juice" Pilshchykov's callsign was given by American colleagues at Clear Sky 2018,
  and for years he was reportedly the only UkrAF pilot with an official one. By the 2030s of
  this fiction, NATO-style R/T is the native register of the force.

## Who is flying this thing

The theatre pivot made the operator concrete: 2030s Ukraine, the extrapolated war (see

A Ukrainian Air Force aviator who was a teenager in Kyiv in 2022, listening to the air-raid

economical, dry, composure held on purpose when the news is bad.

## Why GHOST

The Ghost of Kyiv was a myth about an unbeatable interceptor defending Ukrainian skies —
publicly embraced by the UkrAF as the collective spirit of the 40th Tactical Aviation
Brigade after the pilot behind its most famous image was killed in 2023. The Rapier program
is that myth built into hardware: a jet that appears out of a hole in the ground, kills in
one pass, and vanishes. The squadron that flies it inherited the name because in this
fiction, Ukraine finally built the thing the story promised. The callsign is the doctrine —
and the doctrine is the elegy.
