# Saucer Springs: the adventure layer

Design draft, 30 Oct 2026. The primary reference is **A Link to the Past (SNES)** and **Ocarina of
Time (N64)**. Everything else is flavour on that structure: Toy Story, Stranger Things and IT's
small-town uncanny, the desert south-west, and an original fantasy spirit world. The player is the
owner's 6-year-old. The research sources are in `research-zelda.md` and `research-setting.md`.

## The story in one breath

The Air Force has captured some aliens and is holding them in a hangar on Nellis. They are the
**good** aliens: they came to protect us from the **bad** aliens, who want to break into our world
from the **upside-down**. One night a captured alien reaches out with the last of its power, and the
glow wakes a toy: **you**, an RC car on a kid's bedroom floor. The good aliens wake toys; the bad
aliens' signal takes them over. So there are **good toys and bad toys**. To free the aliens and
stop the break-in, you cross the base, climb **the mountain** where the river begins, and pass
through its old gate into the upside-down. The creatures of the mountain help a toy that carries the
good aliens' light.

## The toy's forms are the Zelda items

Lesson from Majora's Mask: each form must move differently and have routes only it can use, and the
world shows where each is needed. Lesson from Transformers: forms that handle alike add nothing. Lesson
from the Water Temple: **switching must be instant**, one button, never a menu.

| Form | Where you get it | What only it can do | World signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RC car** (start) | You wake as one | Speed, jumps, boat mode in water, Enter-flight (later) | Roads, ramps, rivers |
| **Walking robot** | Dungeon 1 (the mine) | Climb ledges and ladders, push and pull, crawl through gaps, flip switches, talk to toys | Ledges with handholds, crawl gaps under doors |
| **Skateboard** | Dungeon 2 (the school or a skate site) | Grind rails across gaps, wall-ride, THPS tricks open combo doors | Rails across pits, quarter-pipes up to high doors |
| **Flying suit** | Dungeon 3 (the mountain lookout) | Glide from high places, hover over hazards, ride updrafts in the valleys | Updraft vents, cliff edges with far landings |

**Scale.** It's all one toy-scale world, so the forms don't change your size. The robot simply gets
into the toy-sized places: under picnic tables, through skirting-board gaps, into drains and drawers.
That's Minish Cap's idea without its shrink portals.

## Dungeons: real-ish places

Ocarina's rule, kept for a 6-year-old: **one locked door at a time**, so a key is never wasted.
Each dungeon follows ALttP's loop: find its form or gadget halfway → use it on the rest → beat the
boss with it → take it out into the world, where it opens new routes.

**First act** (Light World, about 20–30 minutes each, the length of OoT's Deku Tree):
1. **The Old Silver Mine.** A Nelson/Techatticup-style abandoned mine in the hills: mine-cart rails,
   rusty trucks, timber shafts. You find the **walking robot** form. The boss is a bad toy excavator,
   beaten by climbing onto it. You free alien 1.
2. **Saucer Springs Elementary after dark.** Stranger Things corridors, the gym, the boiler room. You
   find the **skateboard**. The boss is a taken-over kickball bot in the gym. You free alien 2.
3. **The Lookout on the mountain's shoulder.** A ruined fire lookout and slot canyons with real
   flash floods ("sky goes dark, get to high ground"). You find the **flying suit**. The boss is a
   giant possessed kite. You free alien 3. With three aliens free, the guardians open the gate at
   the summit.

**Upside-down** (the world flips, as in ALttP after the pendants): the same base and mountain, taken
over by bad-alien machinery. 5–7 dungeons (Zelda used 7; start with 3 and grow):
- **The Fossil Cave** (Gypsum Cave inspired): ground-sloth bones and fossil dung (a 6-year-old's
  favourite fact), crystal formations; a scorpions-under-blacklight puzzle.
- **The Drowned Town** (inspired by the Lost City under Lake Mead): flip the reservoir level, the one
  big puzzle-box dungeon, made with instant form switching so it isn't a Water Temple.
- **The Flooded Arroyo / Rouge River**: boats, currents, the train bridge as a boss arena.
- **The BX Basement and the Hangar Complex**: bad-alien factory turning toys bad; the final rescue.
- **The Final Tower**: the bad aliens' machine forcing the gate open.

## A working drone-era base

Nellis flies both manned jets (4th, 5th and 6th generation: F-15s and F-16s, F-22s and F-35s,
and a sleek 6th-gen prototype) and lots of drones. The flyovers already show the manned side.
Drones bring the base to life and give it its first-act threat:
- **Security bots** on the ground: wheeled patrol bots with search beams, gate sentries, robot
  dogs. Stealth sections in the style of ALttP's castle guards and OoT's courtyard: stay out of the
  beam or get chased back to the last checkpoint (never a game over).
- **Quadcopter drones** that hover and follow, and sentry towers that sweep.
- **Big drones** (MQ-9 style) circling overhead and taxiing on the runway, as set dressing and
  as obstacles.
- Under the corrupted leadership the bots get **meaner**: red lights, faster patrols. In the
  upside-down, some are fused with bad-alien tech.

## Enemies: good toys and bad toys

- **Everyday bad toys** (tin soldiers, wind-up monkeys, RC tanks, jack-in-the-box door guards) **pop
  apart** into springs, cogs and confetti. They're just toys; it's cartoony.
- **Named toys and bosses** get **freed**: the bad signal breaks, they shake it off and join your
  crew. Your crew grows as you play; the race rivals and playmates are half-taken-over toys you
  win over.
- **Combat** uses Ocarina's Z-targeting idea: lock on, and the enemies take turns. It's simple
  enough for a kid, and it came from ninja films.

## Tone

Spooky-fun, never horror. Stranger Things' bikes-and-flashlights, kids-against-the-uncanny feeling,
and IT's small town with something wrong underneath, at a level that wouldn't make a 6-year-old cry
(Re-Volt did). No gore, no clowns. The upside-down is weird and wrong (static, inverted colours,
toys that move jerkily), not gruesome.

## The setting: real land, invented spirits

This is where the research matters most. The land is real; the magic is invented.

**Real and welcome** (the "land layer"): the geology, animals and history around Nellis.
- Red-rock canyons (Valley of Fire style), Joshua trees, creosote, cholla, wildflower blooms,
  monsoon storms and flash floods.
- Desert creatures as friends and guides: a slow, wise **desert tortoise** (you help it, never carry
  it, which is the real rule), roadrunner, kit fox, jackrabbit, bighorn, burrowing owl, chuckwalla.
  A Gila monster you photograph but don't touch, also the real rule.
- Abandoned mines, ghost towns, mine carts; Area 51 kitsch, the Extraterrestrial Highway, atomic-age
  Las Vegas, Nellis and the Thunderbirds.

**Invented** (the "fiction layer"): the aliens, the toys, the upside-down, and the **spirit world**.
The mountain's guardians are the desert's creatures and elements, an original fantasy: a tortoise
elder, a raven, the wind in the slot canyon, the spring where the river begins. Because you're a
tiny toy, you talk with these non-human beings, not with people, and that is how you cross over.

**What we don't do,** following Native developers and critics (LaPensée; the Never Alone
partnership) and the tribes' own words:
- **The mountain is invented and unnamed**, not a real one. The Spring Mountains (*Nuvagantu*) are
  the Southern Paiute creation site, and the Sheep Range (*Nah'gah Kai*) is a sacred landscape the
  tribe says is being harmed by the bombing range. Making a real sacred site the aliens' portal
  would land squarely on that grievance.
- **No blending of the aliens with Native imagery.** "Petroglyphs are aliens" is the ancient-aliens
  trope that erases the people who made them. Rock art can appear as protected scenery you look at
  and never touch or decode, which is the real park rule.
- **No real stories, songs or figures.** No Coyote (Southern Paiute Coyote stories are told only in
  winter), no chants, no ceremonies, no sacred objects as loot. No pan-Indian props (tipis,
  headdresses, dreamcatchers).
- **An optional respectful acknowledgement** on the title or credits screen ("This is Nuwuvi land:
  the Southern Paiute have lived here since time immemorial"), linking to the tribes' own sites.
- **If we ever want real Paiute culture in the game, we do it as a partnership:** contact the Las Vegas
  Paiute Tribe and the Moapa Band, and pay a Southern Paiute adviser or artist.

## The first slice to build

One complete loop that proves the idea, about 15 minutes:
1. **The opening.** The alien's glow wakes your RC car in a bedroom; drive under the door, across the
   base at night to the hangar; see the captured aliens; the guards come; escape. This is new content
   on existing base geometry.
2. **The mountain's foot.** Drive to a mine entrance in the hills; transform into the robot for the
   first time at a crawl gap. This is the car-to-robot handoff.
3. **The Old Silver Mine**, a compact first dungeon: 6–8 rooms, 2 small keys, one locked door at a
   time, mine-cart ride, the excavator boss, free alien 1.

Technical prerequisites, in order:
1. A **walking robot controller**: a capsule character with climb, crawl and push, sharing the
   physics world and the chase camera.
2. **Instant form switching**, a single key, with the car/robot state handed over cleanly.
3. **Interiors with doors and keys**: a small dungeon framework (rooms, small keys, a big key, one
   open lock at a time, a map and a compass).
4. **Lock-on combat** with enemies taking turns, and the pop-apart and freed toy reactions.
5. **The mountain terrain and the river** from it (boat mode is already built).
