## 1. World structure

### A Link to the Past (SNES, 1991)

- **The two maps share one layout.** The Dark World is stored in the ROM "only as an overlay on the Light World, since their layouts are nearly identical." The cartridge was 8 Mbit, twice the 4 Mbit that most SNES games of the time used. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past))
  - *Mechanism:* one piece of geography teaches the player both maps. A rock that cannot be passed in one world is a cliff edge or a pit in the other, so the player solves the route by checking the matching spot across worlds.
- **The Magic Mirror only goes one way.** Portals take Link into the Dark World. He can only leave by using the Mirror, which leaves a sparkling return portal behind; using the Mirror again in the Dark World erases it. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past)) The Mirror comes from the Light World's Death Mountain. ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past/Items))
  - *Mechanism:* you mirror out from a spot in the Dark World that you could not otherwise reach, and you land at the matching spot in the Light World, often an isolated ledge.
- **Items act as keys to the overworld** ([Wikibooks item list](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past/Items)):

| Item | Where it is found | What it opens up |
|---|---|---|
| Pegasus Boots | Sahasrahla, after the Eastern Palace | Dashing; breaking into secrets |
| Power Glove | Desert Palace | Lifting light rocks |
| Moon Pearl | Tower of Hera | Keeping Link's true form in the Dark World (without it he is a helpless rabbit **[unverified]**) |
| Magic Hammer | Palace of Darkness | Pounding down stakes |
| Hookshot | Swamp Palace | Crossing pits |
| Titan's Mitt | Thieves' Town ("Gargoyle Dungeon") | Lifting dark rocks |
| Flippers | Zora's Domain | Swimming |
| Flute | Haunted Grove | Warping around the map |
| Ether / Quake medallions | Ether: Death Mountain; Quake: Dark World ring of rocks | "Sometimes unlock new paths" |
| Bottles | Four in total | Holding potions, fairies, bees |

  In vanilla the medallions gate Misery Mire and Turtle Rock (Ether and Quake respectively) **[unverified]**.
- **The hub.** Kakariko, Link's house and Hyrule Castle sit in the middle of the map, and the Pyramid of Power is the Dark World's centre **[unverified]**. The first dungeon is Hyrule Castle, played on a fixed path. ([Zelda Dungeon walkthrough index](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/Zelda03HeartContainers.php))

### Ocarina of Time (N64, 1998)

- **Hyrule Field is the central hub,** with the outlying regions joined to it. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time)) Miyamoto first wanted a first-person view so players could take in the field's terrain. He dropped it once Link became a child who had to be visible on screen (same source).
- **The time split is the game's "two worlds."** Pulling the Master Sword moves Link seven years forward. Adult Link can use the Fairy Bow; child Link fits through small passages. Once certain tasks are done, putting the sword back or pulling it again switches between the two ages. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time))
  - *Mechanism:* the same map, but each age has its own items. Child-only: Slingshot, Boomerang, Fairy Ocarina. Adult-only: Fairy Bow, Hookshot, Longshot, Megaton Hammer. ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time/Items))
- **Each region is themed around its dungeon** (pairings below from Wikibooks, Wikipedia and my own knowledge):
  - Kokiri Forest: Deku Tree (Slingshot)
  - Death Mountain / Goron City: Dodongo's Cavern (Bomb Bag)
  - Zora's Domain: Jabu-Jabu's Belly (Boomerang)
  - Adult temples: Forest (Fairy Bow), Fire (Megaton Hammer), Water (Longshot), Shadow, Spirit.
  - Kakariko: Bottom of the Well (Lens of Truth).
  - Shadow Temple gives the Hover Boots; the Ice Cavern gives the Iron Boots; the Spirit Temple gives the Silver Gauntlets (child) and the Mirror Shield (adult) **[unverified]**.
  - The adult Hookshot is not a temple item: it comes from Dampé's grave. ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time/Items))

## 2. Dungeon design

- **The formula.** In ALttP, dungeons are "often multi-level," and each has a large chest whose item is usually required to finish the game. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past)) Brown's *Boss Keys* says ALttP is where "Nintendo really established the formula." ([Amara listing of the episode](https://amara.org/v/C3BF5)) The loop:
  1. Find the dungeon item partway through.
  2. Use it on the second half's puzzles.
  3. Use it on the boss, as its weak point.
  4. Take it out to the overworld, where it opens new areas.

  For example, the Hookshot from the Swamp Palace then crosses overworld gaps.
- **The pieces every dungeon has:** Small Keys, a Big Key, a Map and a Compass. ([Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past/Items))
  - Small keys are spent and gone.
  - The Big Key opens the big chest and the boss door **[unverified]**.
  - The Map shows the rooms; the Compass shows where the boss and chests are.
- **The lock-and-key graph.** According to coverage of Brown's OoT episode, there is "only one locked door available at any given time," so the player never has to choose where to spend a key. Most OoT dungeons amount to "pick up a key, find the lock in the next room." The Water Temple is the exception: you change the water level, which reshapes the whole dungeon. Brown calls it the start of the "puzzle-box" style of dungeon. ([Zelda Dungeon interview with Brown](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/?p=24248); [Nerdist](https://nerdist.com/article/what-are-the-best-dungeons-in-legend-of-zelda-and-why/))
  - *For a 6-year-old:* use one open lock at a time. That is the OoT model, and it means a key can never be wasted.
- **How many dungeons:**
  - ALttP: Hyrule Castle (prologue), 3 Light World dungeons (Eastern Palace, Desert Palace, Tower of Hera), Hyrule Castle Tower, 7 Dark World dungeons and Ganon's Tower. That makes 10 boss Heart Containers. ([Zelda Dungeon, heart pieces](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/Zelda03HeartContainers.php))
  - OoT: 3 child dungeons, then the adult temples plus mini-dungeons. The Deku Tree can be cleared in about 10 minutes. ([Zelda Dungeon search snippet](https://zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Ocarina_of_Time_Dungeons))
- **Rooms per dungeon:** I could not verify typical room counts.
- **How difficulty ramps:** early dungeons are short and linear. Later ones stack mechanics (Water Temple water levels, Lens of Truth invisibility in Shadow and Spirit) and use more floors. This is my synthesis of the sources above, not a sourced claim.

## 3. Core loop and moment-to-moment

- **Z-targeting and why it exists.** Koizumi: without it "the enemies would swarm the player all at once, so it would be a mess." The idea came from a ninja show at Toei Kyoto Studio Park, where the ninjas attacked the hero one at a time. "Z Targeting flags one particular opponent, telling the other enemies to wait." Osawa added an invisible "kusarigama-like tether" so Link can circle the enemy he has locked on to. It also fixed a Mario 64 problem, where trying to read a sign meant "go[ing] around it in circles." ([Iwata Asks, OoT 3D, vol. 2 part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-2-Original-Development-Staff-Part-1/3-Let-s-Go-to-Toei-Kyoto-Studio-Park-/3-Let-s-Go-to-Toei-Kyoto-Studio-Park--231680.html))
  - The marker was redesigned as a fairy, which became Navi. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/z-targeting-in-the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-was-inspired-by-ninjas/))
  - It was patented as JP 9-298111, filed in October 1997 ([Wikipedia: Yoshiaki Koizumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshiaki_Koizumi)).
  - *For a child:* lock-on plus "enemies take turns" is the most transferable idea in this brief.
- **One context-sensitive button.** A single button does many actions, and the on-screen prompt changes with the situation (OoT). Projectiles aim themselves while locked on. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time))
- **Hearts:**
  - ALttP: start with 3 hearts, maximum 20. There are 24 Pieces of Heart, 4 to a container. ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/Zelda03HeartContainers.php))
  - OoT: 36 Pieces, adding 9 containers. ([GameWith](https://gamewith.net/zelda-ocarina-of-time/76407))
- **Rupees:** the Deku Shield costs 40 rupees, earned by cutting grass. That is the first economy lesson. ([Zelda Universe walkthrough](https://zeldauniverse.net/guides/ocarina-of-time/walkthrough/inside-the-deku-tree/))
- **Bottles** hold potions, fairies, bees and fish. ALttP has 4. OoT also has 4, but only one is needed to progress. ([ALttP Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past/Items); [OoT Wikibooks](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time/Items))
- **Secrets:** the Pegasus Boots "break into many secrets." Bombable walls and OoT's hidden grottos are well-known staples **[unverified, no source fetched]**.
- **Side quests:**
  - OoT's adult trading sequence is 12 trades long and ends with the unbreakable Biggoron Sword. Some steps are timed, and the last is a claim check collected after 3 days. Child Link's trading chain ends with the Mask of Truth. ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/wiki/Ocarina_of_Time_Trading_Sequence); [GameWith](https://gamewith.net/zelda-ocarina-of-time/76407))
  - The Skulltula hunt has 100 Gold Skulltulas. The reward tiers are **[unverified]**.
- **The ocarina is a verb.** There are 12 songs built from 5 notes. They solve music puzzles and warp Link to places he has already visited. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time))
- **Epona** is faster than walking, but Link can only use arrows while riding. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Ocarina_of_Time)) The game also restricts where she can go **[unverified]**.

## 4. Pacing and onboarding

- **ALttP's opening: a rainy night.**
  - Lightning frames the uncle as he leaves the house, and Link's body language carries the mood without dialogue. ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2019/05/08/tingles-maps-hyrule-castle-a-link-to-the-past/))
  - Guards teach bush-lifting as a dare: "You look strong for a kid. How much can you lift?"
  - The core elements arrive "one at a time without ever requiring a little fairy… to yell at you." ([Kotaku AU](https://www.kotaku.com.au/?p=873909); [Zelda Dungeon, "On Introductions"](https://zeldadungeon.net/?p=53615))
  - *Mechanism:* a fixed route through the castle and sewers, getting the sword and lamp along the way, and a rescue as the first goal.
- **OoT's opening: Kokiri Forest.**
  - Mido blocks the path to the Deku Tree until Link has the Kokiri Sword (found in a crawlspace along the boulder path) and the Deku Shield (40 rupees from the shop).
  - So the hub itself teaches crawling, cutting grass, the economy and shopping before the first dungeon. ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/guides/ocarina-of-time/walkthrough/inside-the-deku-tree/); [Zelda Universe, Kokiri map](https://zeldauniverse.net/2020/05/06/tingles-maps-kokiri-forest/))
  - Navi does chime in to explain specific actions, which critics count against it.
- **Structure:**
  - ALttP: 3 pendants, then the Master Sword, then Agahnim, then 7 crystals. The Master Sword "requires three pendants." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_A_Link_to_the_Past))
  - OoT: 3 Spiritual Stones as a child, then the 7-year jump, then the adult medallion temples.
  - The same shape in both: a short, safe first act, then the world flips and opens up.
- **Hours** (these are secondary sites citing HowLongToBeat; I could not reach HLTB directly):
  - ALttP main story: about 15–17.5 h ([Nintenderos](https://www.nintenderos.com/2024/06/the-legend-of-zelda-esta-es-la-duracion-de-los-juegos-de-la-saga-del-mas-corto-al-mas-extenso/)).
  - OoT main story: about 26–27 h ([Orbis Patches](https://orbispatches.com/gaming-faq/how-long-would-it-take-to-play-every-legend-of-zelda-game)).

## 5. What worked for kids, and what frustrated players

- **The exploration premise.** Miyamoto explored the countryside around Sonobe as a child. He found a pitch-black cave and came back with a lantern to explore it. ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/features/zelda-at-40-how-shigeru-miyamotos-childhood-explorations-inspired-nintendos-legendary-classic); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Miyamoto)) The story is secondhand; I found no verbatim interview quote.
- **The Water Temple as Nintendo's own failure case** ([Iwata Asks, OoT 3D, vol. 4 part 3](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Iwata-Asks/Iwata-Asks-The-Legend-of-Zelda-Ocarina-of-Time-3D/Vol-4-Development-Staff/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple-/3-I-ve-Got-to-Fix-the-Water-Temple--235888.html)):
  - Iwata: "like a bone caught in your throat for 13 years."
  - Aonuma: "The worst thing was thinking about how many people may have given up there."
  - The 3DS fix was to let Link put on and take off the Iron Boots "in a flash."
  - Aonuma elsewhere said he was most sorry that the boots were hard to toggle. ([Wikipedia: Water Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Temple_(Ocarina_of_Time)))
  - **Lesson:** the frustration came from menu friction on a mechanic used constantly, plus a dungeon whose state the player had lost track of. It was not about the puzzle being hard.
- **Hints in the remake.** OoT 3D added hint movies (Sheikah Stones) because players got stuck. ([Nintendo Everything](https://nintendoeverything.com/ocarina-of-time-3d-discusses-hint-movies-save-system-debate)) The Iwata Asks page I fetched does not mention this.
- **Getting lost.** Brown's *Minish Cap* episode criticises poor hint delivery. ([Room Escape Artist](https://roomescapeartist.com/2017/09/10/boss-keys-analysis-zelda-dungeons/))
- **Not found:** an official Nintendo statement about designing ALttP or OoT specifically for children.

## 6. Toy-scale precedents

- **Minish Cap** (Capcom/Flagship, GBA, 2004):
  - Everyday objects are portals into the tiny world: tree stumps, cauldrons, and pots, which only work upside down. The Cane of Pacci flips them. ([Zelda Wiki: Minish Portal](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/Minish_Portal); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_The_Minish_Cap))
  - Dungeons use giant barrels, giant spider webs and raft-sized lily pads. Shrinking is allowed only at portals, so the designers control when it happens. ([Nintendo Life retrospective](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2015/01/retrospective_the_legend_of_zelda_the_minish_cap_a_decade_on))
  - *Mechanism:* fixed shrink points turn size into a key, like any other item. I did not find a Flagship design interview.
- **Pikmin:** came out of Miyamoto's gardening and watching ants carry litter (secondhand accounts). "Giant cliffs are footprints"; a paper bag blocks a path and a battery is a resource. ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2013/07/feature_a_brief_history_of_pikmin); [Infendo](https://infendo.com/miyamoto-on-the-origins-of-pikmin/))
- **Grounded** (Obsidian, 2020): ant-sized players in a backyard. A juice box becomes a landmark visible across the map, which helps navigation. ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2020/05/05/looking-into-groundeds-unnatural-wonders); [Wccftech interview with Brennecke](https://wccftech.com/grounded-interview-with-obsidian-game-director-adam-brennecke/amp/))
- **Micro Machines** (Codemasters, 1991): tracks on breakfast tables, pool tables, desks and treehouses, with cereal boxes and pencil sharpeners as obstacles. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_Machines_(video_game)))
  - *Mechanism:* the edge of a familiar surface is the track boundary, so the player reads the layout instantly.
- **Astro Bot** (Team Asobi): Doucet chose separate levels over an open world because levels "gave us the most control over the game's variety." Levels were built from modular segments that could be reused across levels, and a 20-minute level was cut for repeating ideas. ([Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/astro-bot-ditched-open-world-design-to-provide-optimal-variety/amp/); [Edge](https://knowledge.edge-online.com/p/how-did-team-asobi-make-the-best-game-of-2024)) I found no source on Astro Bot's use of everyday objects.
- **Toy Story games and Bugsnax:** not researched. Searches turned up nothing usable.

## 7. Transformations as items

- **Majora's Mask** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Zelda:_Majora%27s_Mask)) has 24 masks; three transform Link, and some areas can only be reached in a transformed form.

| Form | Abilities |
|---|---|
| Deku | Spin attack, bubbles, skipping on water, short flights launched from Deku Flowers |
| Goron | High-speed rolling, heavy punches, walking on lava |
| Zora | Fast swimming, boomerang fins, an electric shield, walking on the bottom of water |

  - *Mechanism:* each form is a full movement set, and the world is scattered with signals that tell you which form a spot needs: a Deku Flower, a lava field, deep water.
  - Development: built on the OoT engine in about 15 months.
- **Transformers: War for Cybertron** (High Moon, 2010):
  - Players switch forms at will by clicking the left stick, and the levels are built around that switch. ([TechEnclave review](https://techenclave.com/t/transformers-war-for-cybertron-review/170255); [Gaming Nexus](https://gamingnexus.com/Article/2674/Transformers-War-for-Cybertron---Hands-on))
  - Critics said the ground vehicles all felt like hovertanks and that levels made too little use of vehicle modes.
  - **Lesson:** if every form handles alike, the switch adds nothing. Each form needs routes that only it can use.
- **Wind Waker:** not researched; I have no sourced facts.

## Design takeaways for the RC game (my own reading, not sourced)

1. **Two worlds, one map.** Build one town map with a matching transformed version (ALttP), or the same map at two scales (Minish Cap).
2. **Each dungeon unlocks a form.** Use the ALttP item loop: car, then robot, then skateboard, then flying suit. Each form solves its own dungeon, beats its boss and opens overworld routes. Give each one a clear world signal, as Majora's Mask does.
3. **One open lock at a time** (OoT), and **no menu friction when switching forms.** The friction is the Water Temple's real lesson.
4. **Lock-on with enemies taking turns** for any combat.
5. **A first act of 3 dungeons, then the world flips,** and teach by gating, not text. Mido's sword-and-shield check is the model.
