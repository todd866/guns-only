# Nellis-area kids' game: cultural and natural research brief

This is web research only; no code or files were changed. Claims carry their source links inline, and a section near the end lists what I could not confirm.

---

## 1. Whose land

**The Southern Paiute (Nuwu / Nuwuvi).** The Las Vegas Paiute Tribe says its ancestors, the Tudinu ("Desert People"), have lived in southern Nevada since at least 1100 AD. Their territory covered the Colorado River area, most of south-eastern Nevada, and parts of California and Utah. The tribe calls the Spring Mountains (Mount Charleston) **Nuvagantu**, "where snow sits", and describes them as the *sacred creation site* of the Southern Paiute ([lvpaiute.gov](https://lvpaiute.gov/)). Some history worth knowing ([lvpaiute.gov](https://lvpaiute.gov/)):
- In 1911 the rancher Helen J. Stewart deeded 10 acres, which became the Las Vegas Paiute Colony.
- In 1965 and 1968 the Southern Paiute bands won settlements for land taken illegally.
- In 1983 Congress added about 4,000 acres at Snow Mountain.

Earlier, the Spanish Trail slave trade hit the Nuwuvi hard. Nuwuvi resistance helped force the 1855 Mormon mission at Las Vegas to leave in 1857 ([UNLV land acknowledgement](https://www.unlv.edu/about/statements-compliance/land-acknowledgement/nuwuvi); [Nuwuvi: A Southern Paiute History, 1976](https://dp.la/item/506a03fcf57c3431d61f2535d3225e18)).

**The Moapa Band of Paiutes** is a federally recognised tribe on the Moapa River Indian Reservation, about 72,000 acres. The reservation was first meant to cover the Moapa watershed and land along the Colorado River, some of it now under Lake Mead. In the 1870s it was cut to about 1,000 acres ([Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moapa_Band_of_Paiute_Indians); [2014 Congressional Record on the Moapa land conveyance](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CRECB-2014-pt8/html/CRECB-2014-pt8-Pg11755.htm)). The tribe's site mentions work to preserve "our legends, songs and dances" ([moapabandofpaiutes.com](https://moapabandofpaiutes.com/)).

**Nellis and its range.** Nellis's own cultural resources programme describes working relationships with Western Shoshone and Southern Paiute tribes over the Nevada Test and Training Range ([DENIX award writeup](https://denix.osd.mil/awards/previous-years/fy12secdef/crmi/nellis-air-force-base-installation/); [Nellis tribal site visit, ACC](https://www.acc.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/2572459/nellis-leaders-tribal-reps-visit-native-american-historical-site/)). The range cost 17 tribes heavily: Southern Paiute, Western Shoshone, Owens Valley Paiute/Shoshone and Fort Mojave ([Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/nellis-air-force-base-drops-expansion-bomb-on-southern-nevada)). Western Shoshone land claims under the 1863 Treaty of Ruby Valley also cover the old Nevada Test Site, and the Shoshone have refused the $26 million claims award ([Cultural Survival](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/node/4581)).

**The most important point for this game:** the Las Vegas Paiute chair and vice-chair have written that the Sheep Range and Desert National Wildlife Refuge are **Nah'gah Kai**, a landscape of "special meaning for our people". They name the destruction of **Pintwater Cave**, "which holds a special place in our religious beliefs and stories", and say "Bombing our sacred sites is the opposite of stewardship." Tribal access is limited to two trips a year of 15 people ([Nevada Independent op-ed](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-desert-refuge-is-sacred-dont-bomb-it)). A cheerful game about the Nellis bombing range sits right on top of this grievance.

**Guidance on media and games:** I found **none** published by either tribe. The Las Vegas Paiute site names its Tribal Administration as the contact: (702) 386-3926, contact@lvpaiute.com.

## 2. Landmarks

| Place | What's really there | Game suitability |
|---|---|---|
| **Valley of Fire** (state park since 1935) | Red Aztec sandstone from the Jurassic. Petroglyphs by the **Basketmaker** culture about 2,500 years ago, then **Early Pueblo** people; Paiutes were living there by 1865 ([NV State Parks](https://parks.nv.gov/parks/valley-of-fire)). Carvings are cut into dark desert varnish. | Great red-rock scenery. Treat the rock art as protected scenery, never as puzzle glyphs to "decode". |
| **Atlatl Rock** | A panel showing an atlatl (spear-thrower), with bighorn above; a metal staircase climbs to it ([Hikespeak](https://www.hikespeak.com/trails/atlatl-rock-hike-valley-of-fire-nevada/)). | A staircase landmark you can look at. |
| **Mouse's Tank** | A natural rain basin (*tinaja*). It is named after "Little Mouse", a Southern Paiute man of the 1890s branded a renegade, hunted by a posse and reportedly shot ([DesertUSA](https://www.desertusa.com/dusablog/legends-of-mice-and-more-bighorn-in-valley-of-fire/); [Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/places/mouses-tank)). The sources are mostly signs and tourism sites. | **Do not use the "Mouse" story.** It is a settler outlaw tale about killing a Native man. A water basin with no backstory is fine. |
| **Elephant Rock, Fire Wave** | Fire Wave trails close every year from 15 May to 30 September because of heat ([NV State Parks](https://parks.nv.gov/parks/valley-of-fire)). | Good set dressing: rock arches and striped sandstone. |
| **Lost City / Pueblo Grande de Nevada** | Ancestral Puebloan villages in the Moapa Valley, occupied for more than 1,000 years. Found by John and Fay Perkins in 1924 and dug by M.R. Harrington; much of it was drowned by Lake Mead ([NPS Lake Mead](https://www.nps.gov/lake/learn/the-lost-city.htm)). The museum building dates from 1935 ([SAH Archipedia](https://sah-archipedia.org/node/16844)). | "A town under the lake" is a strong image. Keep it about homes and farming, not "lost treasure". |
| **Gypsum Cave** | Harrington's 1930 dig found ground-sloth dung, hair and claws; Bertha Parker found the sloth skull. His claim that humans lived alongside ice-age animals was later undercut by radiocarbon dating ([NHM LA](https://nhm.org/gypsum-cave)). A horse from the cave was named *Haringtonhippus* in 2017. | **Excellent for kids**: giant sloths and their fossil poo. A paleontology level carries no cultural risk. |
| **Sloan Canyon NCA** (2002) | More than 300 rock-art panels and about 1,700 design elements, from Archaic to historic times. Hiking only ([BLM](https://blm.gov/visit/sloan-canyon-petroglyphs)). | Scenery only. |
| **Red Rock Canyon** | Agave **roasting pits**: rings of fire-cracked limestone used to cook agave hearts and tortoises. Rock art at Willow Springs ([BLM handout](https://www.blm.gov/sites/blm.gov/files/uploads/Nevada-Red%20Rock%20Canyon-Natural%20World.pdf)). | The roasting pit is a respectful, everyday-life detail: food, not mysticism. |
| **Desert NWR / Sheep Range** (1936) | Set up for desert bighorn. The largest refuge outside Alaska at about 1.6 million acres; visitor centre at Corn Creek ([USFWS](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/desert/about-us)). | Bighorn country, but also **Nah'gah Kai**, a sacred place (see section 1). Show the wildlife, not ceremony or caves. |
| **Gold Butte NM** (2016) | The Falling Man petroglyph and Whitney Pocket rock art ([BLM release](https://www.blm.gov/sites/blm.gov/files/documents/files/2016goldbutte.prc_.rel_.pdf)). | Scenery. |
| **Nelson / Eldorado Canyon** | The Techatticup gold mine was worked from the 1860s to the 1940s. A lawless camp where soldiers were sent to restore order; the mine is now restored for tours ([Travel Nevada](https://travelnevada.com/mines-prospecting/eldorado-canyon-and-techatticup-mine/)). | **Great fun**: mine carts, rusty trucks, a ghost town. |
| **Flash floods** | July to September monsoon. About 80% of Grand Canyon floods arrive between noon and 8pm, and storms 25 miles away can flood a canyon ([Grand Canyon Conservancy](https://www.grandcanyon.org/about/news/6-essential-flash-flood-safety-tips-for-grand-canyon-visitors)). Las Vegas has 722 miles of washes and channels draining to Lake Mead ([News3LV](https://news3lv.com/news/local/story/officials-warn-flash-floods-can-quickly-become-deadly-as-monsoon-season-returns)). | A great mechanic: "the sky goes dark, get to high ground", which also teaches real safety. |

Lake Mead, Hoover Dam and the Muddy and Virgin rivers are the obvious "big water" set pieces. Only the drowning of the Lost City is sourced above.

## 3. Creatures and plants

- **Desert tortoise.** Nevada's state reptile. The Mojave population has been federally *threatened* since 2 April 1990, and harassing or collecting one is illegal ([USFWS 1990](https://www.fws.gov/sites/default/files/documents/historic-news-releases/1990/19900328.pdf); [BLM fact sheet](https://www.blm.gov/sites/blm.gov/files/documents/Nevada_SNDO_Desert_Tortoise_Fact_Sheet_0.pdf)). A frightened tortoise empties its bladder, its water store, which is why you never pick one up ([Wildlife News](https://www.thewildlifenews.com/2014/04/26/desert-tortoise-is-the-nevada-state-reptile/)). **Game rule:** you *help* the tortoise, never carry it. A slow, wise tortoise friend fits Toy Story and Zelda equally well.
- **Gila monster.** Protected under Nevada law (NAC 503.080), so don't touch it. Nevada asks people to photograph sightings and report them ([Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/nevada/NAC-503-080)). A "photo mode" quest suits it perfectly.
- **Desert bighorn.** The reason the Desert NWR exists ([USFWS](https://www.fws.gov/refuge/desert/about-us)), and an animal that matters to Paiute culture ([op-ed](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/the-desert-refuge-is-sacred-dont-bomb-it)).
- **Not individually sourced, but standard Mojave fauna and flora:** chuckwalla (puffs up in crevices), roadrunner, kit fox, black-tailed jackrabbit (giant ears that shed heat), burrowing owl, tarantula (males wander in autumn), bark scorpion (glows under UV light, a lovely "blacklight" mechanic), Joshua tree, creosote (the smell of rain), barrel and cholla cactus ("jumping" cholla), spring wildflowers. The Valley of Fire's plant list confirms creosote, cholla, beavertail, brittlebush, desert marigold and mallow ([NV State Parks](https://parks.nv.gov/parks/valley-of-fire)).

## 4. Southern Paiute stories

**What is published:**
- Edward Sapir's *Texts of the Kaibab Paiutes and Uintah Utes* (1930) includes "Wolf and his Brother" and other Coyote myths ([Glottolog](https://glottolog.org/resource/reference/id/182482)).
- The Pahrump Valley Times printed stories from the Chemehuevi storyteller Larry Eddy. One explains why piñon nuts near Mount Charleston are big: Coyote skipped the planting Wolf trusted him with ([PV Times](https://pvtimes.com/community/coyote-character-central-to-southern-paiute-folklore/)).
- The Utah Education Network has Paiute picture booklets, such as "Coyote and Duck" ([UEN PDF](https://www.uen.org/americanindian/tribes/Paiute/books/PaiuteCoyoteandDuck.pdf)).

Coyote (Sinawava) is the trickster and Wolf is his wiser older brother.

**Restrictions:**
- **Coyote stories (*Tookwee'nup*) are told only in winter.** The UEN material says so ([UEN](https://www.uen.org/americanindian/literacy/files/paiute/lessons/Coyote_Duck.pdf)), and Utah's state archives give the window as about October to February ([Utah Archives](https://archives.utah.gov/2017/12/13/coyote-stories-folktales)). A game is played all year, so a retold Coyote story would break the custom most of the time.
- **The Salt Songs are a sacred 142-song cycle,** sung from sunset to sunrise to guide the dead. The recordings are held for Nuwuvi use only, with all rights kept by the Salt Song Trail Project ([Cultural Conservancy](https://www.nativeland.org/salt-song-trail)). **Never use them.** No "Paiute chant" stand-in either.
- **Places named sacred:** Nuvagantu (Spring Mountains), Nah'gah Kai (Sheep Range), Pintwater Cave.

**The safest model is the National Park Service's own practice.** At Bryce Canyon the NPS names the Paiute "Legend People" but deliberately *does not retell the story*: "It is the custom of the Paiutes to tell these stories only during the winter season", and out of respect it does not repeat it ([NPS wayside](https://www.nps.gov/places/000/the-legend-people-wayside.htm)). **My advice:** don't retell, adapt or remix any Paiute story without the tribe's partnership. In particular, don't make Coyote a character in your toy-and-alien world.

## 5. Do's and don'ts from Native developers and critics

**The models:**
- **Never Alone** was built by E-Line Media with Upper One Games, which is owned by the Cook Inlet Tribal Council. About 40 Alaska Native elders, storytellers and community members took part, and the writer was Ishmael Hope (Iñupiaq and Tlingit). It is narrated in Iñupiaq and includes documentary "cultural insights" ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/404439); [ICT](https://ictnews.org/lifestyle/oregon-trail-to-assassins-creed-right-and-wrong-native-american-portrayals-in-video-games/)). The point is that the community was a partner and owner, not a consultant brought in at the end.
- **Elizabeth LaPensée** (Anishinaabe and Métis) wants Indigenous people as lead designers, artists and writers, paid equally, rather than as late consultants ([Indian Tribal Heritage](https://indiantribalheritage.org/?p=25988)). She calls games "sovereign spaces" ([Towards Sovereign Games, 2022](https://just-tech.ssrc.org/?p=9016)) and has proposed a framework for games built on Indigenous culture, SPEAR ([ANTARES, 2020](https://sou.ucs.br/etc/revistas/index.php/antares/article/download/9103/4480)).
- **Assassin's Creed III:** its Mohawk consultant specifically warned against showing **sacred masks** ([ICT](https://ictnews.org/lifestyle/oregon-trail-to-assassins-creed-right-and-wrong-native-american-portrayals-in-video-games/)).

**Concrete pitfalls:**
1. **Pan-Indian mash-ups.** For example, *Brave: A Warrior's Tale* "gives into many pan-Indian stereotypes" and gives its tribe no name ([ICT](https://ictnews.org/lifestyle/oregon-trail-to-assassins-creed-right-and-wrong-native-american-portrayals-in-video-games/)). Here that would mean no tipis, feather headdresses, totem poles, dreamcatchers or kachinas in Paiute country.
2. **Spirit-animal or mystic-shaman tropes.** Mortal Kombat's Nightwolf is the standard example ([ICT](https://ictnews.org/lifestyle/oregon-trail-to-assassins-creed-right-and-wrong-native-american-portrayals-in-video-games/)), as are Native characters cast as "the shamans for the heroes" ([Cultural Survival](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/news/indigenous-representation-video-games)).
3. **Sacred objects as loot**, and rock art as collectibles or puzzle keys.
4. **Invented ceremonies, chants and "ancient prophecies".**
5. **Natives as enemies or obstacles**, or as people who exist only in the past. Today's tribes run businesses, hold powwows and are suing over land.
6. **Area 51 mysticism blended with Native imagery.** "Petroglyphs are aliens" is a standard ancient-aliens trope (for instance, a blog post titled "[Frogs or Aliens…](https://wandering-through-time-and-place.com/tag/petroglyphs-in-nevada/)"). It erases the people who made the rock art. **Keep the aliens and the Native material completely separate.**
7. **The "Thunderbird".** The aerobatic team's name borrows a mythic figure from other tribes' traditions. Use the jets; don't add a mythology layer. This is my own inference, not sourced.

## 6. Local pop culture that is fair game

- **Extraterrestrial Highway (SR 375).** Named by the state in February 1996 and tied to the release of *Independence Day*. The Little A'Le'Inn in Rachel is the only motel, cafe and alien-souvenir stop on the road ([Wikipedia: SR 375](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_375); [AARoads](https://www.aaroads.com/guides/nv-375)). This is the right home for every UFO joke.
- **Nellis history.** It opened in 1941 as a gunnery school and took the name of Lt William Harrell Nellis, a P-47 pilot killed in 1944, on 30 April 1950. The Thunderbirds have been based there since 1956, and Red Flag began in 1975 ([Nellis fact sheet](https://www.nellis.af.mil/About/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/284174/)). Red Flag's "your first ten missions, in peacetime, against an aggressor force" maps neatly onto "practice battles with toy robots".
- **Atomic-age Las Vegas.** About 1,000 tests took place at the Nevada Test Site between 1951 and 1992 ([Cultural Survival](https://www.culturalsurvival.org/node/4581)). The kitsch is fair game (googie signs, retro-futurist robots), but bear in mind that the site is on contested Western Shoshone land.
- **Route 66 never ran through Las Vegas.** The nearest points are Kingman and Oatman in Arizona, about 100 miles south ([route66roadtrip.com](https://www.route66roadtrip.com/route-66-side-trip-to-las-vegas-nevada.htm)). Use it as a "road trip south" side area, or not at all.

## Recommended approach

1. **Split the game into two layers.** The *fiction layer* (toy robots, aliens, Area 51, atomic kitsch, Thunderbirds) is entirely invented. The *land layer* (real geology, real animals, real place names, flash floods, mines, fossils) is accurate. Native material belongs **only** to the land layer, and only in non-sacred, everyday form.
2. **What you can include without consultation:** a respectful acknowledgement at the start ("This is Nuwuvi land: the Southern Paiute have lived here since time immemorial") with a link to the tribes' own sites. Real Paiute place names that the tribes publish themselves, such as Nuvagantu. Agave roasting pits and basket-making as daily life. Rock art seen from a distance, protected, and never touched, collected or decoded (teach "look, don't touch", the real park rule).
3. **What you leave out unless the tribe is a partner:** Coyote and other story characters, songs, ceremonies, spirit beings, Pintwater Cave and anything on Nah'gah Kai beyond its wildlife, and the story of Mouse.
4. **If the owner wants more than that,** contact the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe's administration and the Moapa Band, and pay a Southern Paiute adviser or artist, following LaPensée and Never Alone. Nellis's own Native American programme already has tribal contacts.
5. **What a 6-year-old will love and carries no cultural risk:** the tortoise friend you guide but never carry, Gila-monster photo quests, scorpions under UV light, sloth-poo fossils in Gypsum Cave, flash floods ("get to high ground!"), ghost-town mine carts, bighorn on cliffs, and UFOs on the E.T. Highway.

- **Elephant Rock** isn't described on any official page I found.
