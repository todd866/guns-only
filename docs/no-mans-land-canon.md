# No-man's-land canon: the accidental reserve

Date: 2026-07-28
Status: accepted fiction
Setting: late 2030s, synthetic Ukraine theatre

## The one-sentence truth

No-man's-land is an enormous, beautiful accidental nature reserve created by a war system that
treats every unverified human presence as a possible enemy while classifying wildlife as
irrelevant background.

Nature is genuinely healing. Humanity is not.

## What the zone is

The zone is country-scale, not a narrow line of trenches. Years of reliable autonomous
surveillance, passive electronic-warfare sensors, unattended drones, mines, and competing
machine-enforced access rules have made ordinary settlement and industry intolerably dangerous.
People remain at its edges, in registered islands, and along precarious cleared corridors.
Soldiers, reconnaissance teams, downed pilots, medevac crews, and people trying to live outside the
system still enter it.

The technology does not need to be shabby or incompetent. Much of it is excellent. The defining
social fact is a steep authority gradient: central decisions propagate quickly, cleanly, and with
little meaningful right of appeal. Automation makes that gradient efficient rather than humane.

This is fictional worldbuilding, not a forecast, not an account of the current war, and not a
real-world operational model.

## The visual contradiction

The reserve must be beautiful enough that the player initially wants to believe it is benign:

- grass travelling in coherent wind waves;
- forest canopies returning across abandoned parcels;
- wetlands forming in drainage cuts and old impact scars;
- orchards outliving their villages;
- roads narrowing under weeds and young trees;
- wildlife crossing clearings without tactical significance;
- soft evening haze over precise, clinical instrument symbology.

Human absence is never a clean slate. Close inspection reveals why the land is empty: interrupted
roads, roofs at the perimeter, abandoned utility poles, fenced corridors, warning markers, quiet
recovery sites, and machine infrastructure that is maintained better than the communities around
it.

The landscape is not a universal ruin texture. Most frames should contain living land, not burning
wreckage. A rare smoke column or damaged machine matters because the rest of the view is quiet.

## Moral rules

1. **Beauty is not absolution.** Ecological recovery does not make displacement, contamination,
   mines, or automated violence good.
2. **Wildlife is not an enemy class.** Ambient animals are not targetable, score-bearing, or
   combat props.
3. **Humans are not red dots by nature.** The system may classify them that way; the game must
   preserve uncertainty and consequence rather than endorsing the classification.
4. **No celebratory aftermath.** Destruction is brief, factual, and followed by smoke, silence,
   absence, or a recovery problem.
5. **No gore is needed.** Tragedy can be shown through empty places, interrupted routines,
   damaged tools, medevac pressure, and people deciding whether to help.
6. **Do not copy a particular film.** The influence is painterly light, humane pacing, mechanical
   specificity, environmental attention, and quiet space.

## Where people live

Human life concentrates in three forms:

- **Edge villages:** ordinary communities pressed against the reserve boundary.
- **Registered islands:** clinics, compounds, strips, or municipal nodes protected by negotiated
  access and machine-readable status.
- **Ghost habitation:** people living quietly inside the zone, often outside recognized authority
  and therefore at acute risk from any emission or visible routine.

Local politics matters because safe recovery is social infrastructure. A downed pilot's capsule is
physically survivable; whether a recovery drone gets a quiet landing area, an early warning, or
silence from nearby residents depends on relationships built before the crash.

## Gameplay consequences

- Long-range Rapier flight sees the reserve first as sublime scale and clean geometry.
- Low-altitude flight reveals wind, vegetation, drowned infrastructure, and the fragility of safe
  corridors.
- Indoor fibre-drone reconnaissance reveals the abandoned human interior of the same world.
- Emitting radio energy can wake a quiet sector and turn a survey into a clock.
- Medevac and clinic stories reveal who lives at the edge and who falls out of the sky.
- Later command play must treat access, recovery, medicine, and force as one coupled system.

The player may begin as part of the machinery that maintains the empty green zone. The longer arc
is learning what the green emptiness cost, who still lives inside it, and what authority can do
when it has almost no friction.
